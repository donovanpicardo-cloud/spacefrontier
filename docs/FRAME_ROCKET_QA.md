# Frame pacing, manual camera, expanded supplies and Earthbound QA

Updated 3 November 2026. This document supersedes the previous phase’s adaptive-resolution description and its limited preview-only performance evidence.

## Implemented

- Fraction-preserving render pacing. Synthetic 60/120/144/165/240 Hz refresh tests retain 60 submitted frames per second in the School preset; the old reset-on-render approach dropped to 48 at 144 Hz. Stalls do not trigger catch-up bursts.
- Immutable source meshes are detached after batching instead of remaining invisible in the active scene graph. Frozen world matrices remain usable for camera ray tests; collision shapes captured before batching remain physical. Child attachments retain world transforms. Disposal includes detached geometries/materials. Indexed batches keep all triangles, normals and UV seams, without decimation.
- Stable preset resolution: slow scheduling no longer causes automatic scene blurring. Existing texture assets, shadow resolutions, lighting presets and detailed models remain. Shader preparation uses Three’s compileAsync before accepting a new/continued game, with a loading message. Tab visibility resets render pacing and clock samples instead of including minutes of background inactivity in the next FPS sample.
- Development-only local frame reports: rolling CPU stages, frame percentiles, optional GPU timer queries, exact render dimensions, and shader backend where the browser exposes it. Recent gameplay is retained when paused; history is written under diagnostics/. Multi-pass counters accumulate actual render work rather than reporting only the final full-screen triangle.
- Objective guidance does not rotate the camera. The yellow trail remains visible. Walking, driving, exiting a buggy and choosing a supply destination do not steer toward a goal. Right-drag orbits during repairs, resource recovery, sample/robot activities and airlock transit; left-drag operates repair equipment. Scroll zooms; R explicitly resets the view. Existing collision avoidance can pull the camera closer at walls.
- Solar wires rest in short, separate loops, with a scrambled matching socket order. Connected routes use gently separated depths. Indoor repairs retain two hydroponics handwheels and the replacement filter; dust remains an outdoor solar activity.
- Twelve finite resource sites per planet, four per type. New sites extend to remote terrain around 150–200 m away. Map bounds include them. Foundations are flattened over enough terrain vertices to avoid buried/floating rigs, and remote equipment is included in physical collision collection. Original depletion IDs survive. Larger remote salvage caches provide construction margin; the game requires enough storage for a full cache before beginning extraction so finite yields are not silently discarded.
- Earthbound construction: three visible rocket stages assembled at the habitat fabricator. Total cost: 6 parts, 34% power, 24% water, 12% oxygen, 8% food and one sample research. Complete the five outpost missions, walk to the physical pad, park/exit the buggy, then board. Moon/Mars rockets have distinct accents/engine arrangements and ascent motion.
- A fictional 36-second rendered finale: countdown, ascent from the current outpost, departure planet, engine plume, stage separation, flight toward a rotating NASA Blue Marble Earth with an atmospheric rim, then a saved homecoming end screen. No new WebGL context is created. Space scenery is allocated only for launch, with bounded geometry and one star-particle batch. Pause freezes the timeline and resource simulation; reduced-motion mode removes plume pulsing. Journey length/trajectory/fuel processing are clearly labelled fiction. Saves without a rocket remain compatible; assembled rockets resume ready on the pad, and arrived saves open the end screen.
- Local checkpoints persist on page hide/exit as well as the existing save controls and periodic saves. Tutorial practice does not overwrite the real expedition.

## Actual browser observations, not a cross-browser performance guarantee

- Native Brave was inspected before the fixes: its Settings showed 9 FPS, 345 draw calls and about 488k submitted triangles in the School preset. This was a retained gameplay FPS field inside a paused menu, so it does not establish a continuous steady-state sample.
- A local preview baseline report during active play recorded 240 samples at 59.99 FPS, approximately 2.45 ms CPU and 1.51 ms GPU time, at 745×857 internal resolution in School mode. This desktop has an AMD Radeon RX 7800 XT. That is not a school-computer measurement and does not invalidate the user’s reported lag.
- A later preview sample used Ultra at about 239 FPS on the desktop; its old multi-pass counters reported only the final pass. These were fixed, and that sample is not used as an accurate scene draw count.
- Brave subsequently produced an early menu report with only five frames: roughly 123 ms average CPU and a 557 ms maximum/95th-percentile render-submission stall. Its first interval included roughly 317 seconds of background inactivity. GPU timing was unavailable and the exposed renderer string was “Brave”; this does not prove software rendering or disabled acceleration. Shader prewarming and visibility timing were addressed after this report.
- Native control then repeatedly returned “Computer Use was stopped by the user with the physical Escape key,” including after the user authorised resumption. No further UI input, settings changes or shutdown was attempted through another mechanism.
